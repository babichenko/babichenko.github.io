Yesterday, I completed my first hackathon. I came into it not knowing any other participants and finished with a strong group and a project that I was proud of.

![Presenting the project](/images/blog/barreleye-lens-hackathon-presentation.jpg)

We used ArcGIS by Esri to build a project that visualized environmental data across California for people to make informed decisions about pollution and unsafe water conditions in their communities. We used authoritative data sets from California Open Data and those provided by the platform.

Our project has an interactive map with layer toggles for sea conditions, protected areas, algal blooms, and other features that visualize issues on our coast. It's called "Barreleye Lens", named after the barreleye fish whose transparent head and tubular eyes allow it to observe and filter the environment to see things that other creatures cannot.

We are especially proud of our community post integration that scrapes social media posts relating to the environment and places them onto the map. This allows people to get curated information from the community alongside what is reported by organizations.

Our group of three spent 24 hours in the campus computer science building hacking until submission. We took advantage of the presentations to learn about how to make our project more efficient through the use of other programs. We survived off of pizza, chips, energy drinks, and our love of the game. 

I learned that managing data quality and integration makes or breaks a project. 

For the next competition, I will focus on building a fully usable project before expanding complex features and data pipelines. I am excited to learn and apply these lessons to what comes next.

See the project and code on GitHub: [Barreleye Lens](https://github.com/JonathanBarsoum/Fully-hacks-2026/blob/main/README.md)
