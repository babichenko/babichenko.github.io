It’s official! I have graduated from Orange Coast College with my Associate in Science in Mathematics for Transfer.

In my last semester of high school, I took an introductory course in kinesiology. I fell in love with it. I saw a future where I could do meaningful work that helped people in a direct way. I spent the next two years finishing my general education requirements, working as a physical therapy aide, and taking many of the major-specific kinesiology classes.

That experience taught me something significant: I was not a fan of anatomy. I could dissect problems, but human beings were where I drew the line. 

I pivoted into mathematics, my favorite subject in high school. Math let me build the problem-solving skills I would need for wherever I ended up. I learned how to take complicated ideas and break them down into workable pieces, even when the direction wasn’t clear from the start.

Looking back, the path I took made sense.

My maternal grandmother was a doctor and my paternal grandfather worked with his hands. This explains why I was drawn to physical therapy. At the same time, my paternal grandmother was a math teacher, my maternal grandfather was an inventor, and both of my parents are computer programmers.

I knew I would end up somewhere in the mix of it all.

My brother and I are the first in our family to be born in America. In a month, he will be graduating with his PhD in Mathematics from UC Santa Barbara.

I will be transferring to California State University, Fullerton to pursue my bachelor’s degree in Computer Science.

Just like my parents.

To me, computer science bridges these parts of my journey: problem solving, building tools, and helping people. I want to explore the technology that surrounds accessibility. We are living in the future, but our assistive technology feels stuck in the past. I want to help change that.

Thank you Orange Coast College and everyone who was part of this chapter. I’m excited to be starting at CSUF!
